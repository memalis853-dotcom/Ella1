## 🌅 Breakfast — Boiled Yam with Ugu & Tomato Sauce

**Ingredients**
- ½ medium yam, peeled and sliced
- 2 cups ugu (pumpkin leaves) or spinach, chopped
- 3 fresh tomatoes, chopped
- 1 onion, sliced
- 2 tbsp unrefined red palm oil
- Pinch of salt, ¼ tsp ground crayfish (optional)

**Steps**
1. Boil the yam in salted water for 15–20 minutes until soft.
2. Warm the palm oil gently (do not let it smoke). Add onion, cook 2 minutes.
3. Add tomatoes and crayfish. Cook 5 minutes.
4. Stir in the ugu. Cook 2 minutes, then turn off the heat.
5. Serve the sauce over the yam.

**Why it helps:** Yam is a root-vegetable carb that gives steady energy. Ugu is a leafy green full of iron and magnesium.

**Swap it:** No yam? Use sweet potato. 🇰🇪🇿🇦 Kenya/South Africa: use sukuma wiki or morogo instead of ugu. 🇬🇧🇨🇦🇺🇸 UK/Canada/US: use spinach or kale.

## ☀️ Lunch — Okro Soup with Chicken & Pounded Yam

**Ingredients**
- 500 g chicken, cut into pieces
- 15 fresh okro, chopped or grated
- 1 cup ugu or spinach, chopped
- 1 onion, 2 seasoning peppers (ata rodo), 1 tbsp ground crayfish
- 2 tbsp red palm oil, salt to taste
- Pounded yam (or amala / plantain fufu) to serve

**Steps**
1. Season the chicken with onion and salt. Cook in 2 cups of water for 20 minutes.
2. Add palm oil, pepper and crayfish. Cook 5 minutes.
3. Add the okro. Cook 3–4 minutes only — it stays green and fresh.
4. Add the ugu, stir, and turn off the heat.
5. Serve with pounded yam.

**Why it helps:** Okro is soft and gentle on the gut and helps keep things moving (good for constipation).

**Kid tip 👶:** Picky eater? Blend the okro smooth so there are no "slimy bits" to see.

## 🌙 Dinner — Coriander Pepper Fish with Boiled Green Plantain

**Ingredients**
- 4 small mackerel (titus) or 4 tilapia, cleaned
- 3 green (unripe) plantains
- 1 handful fresh coriander, 2 cloves garlic, 1 small onion
- 1 scotch bonnet (leave out for young children)
- 1 tbsp extra virgin olive oil, juice of 1 lemon, salt

**Steps**
1. Blend coriander, garlic, onion, pepper, olive oil, lemon and salt into a green paste.
2. Rub the paste all over the fish. Rest for 15 minutes.
3. Bake at 200 °C / 400 °F for 20–25 minutes (no frying, no foil — use a baking tray or parchment paper).
4. While it bakes, peel and boil the plantains for 20 minutes.
5. Serve fish with plantain and sliced cucumber.

**Why it helps:** Small fish are lower in mercury than tuna. Green plantain feeds the good bacteria in the gut.

## 🧃 Detox Day 1 — The Daily Detox Smoothie *(author's recipe)*

**Ingredients**
- 1 celery stick
- ½ green apple
- Handful of blueberries
- 1 tbsp fresh coriander (cilantro)
- 1 tbsp fresh parsley
- 1 cup of water (or more)

**Steps:** Blend everything until smooth. Drink fresh, in the morning.

**Good for:** gentle daily cleansing, hydration, and fibre to keep the bowels regular. Celery is the base; apple gives fibre; coriander and parsley are traditional cleansing herbs; blueberries are rich in antioxidants.

**Go slowly:** Start with half a cup for children. Detox should be gentle — if your child gets tummy upset or irritable, use less and build up.

## 🎒 Lunch Box Day 1 — Sweet Potato Chicken Bites *(nut-free)*

**Pack:**
- 6–8 baked sweet potato & chicken bites (made the night before)
- Cucumber sticks
- Mango slices
- Small pot of coconut yogurt for dipping

**Make the bites (night before):** Mash 1 boiled sweet potato with 1 cup cooked, shredded chicken, a pinch of salt and thyme, and 2 tbsp tapioca flour. Roll into small balls, flatten, bake at 200 °C / 400 °F for 20 minutes. Cool and refrigerate.

**Parent tip 💛:** Pack one food your child already loves next to the new food. No pressure — just let it sit there.
